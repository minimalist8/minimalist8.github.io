## Why I Wrote This

I grew up in an Evangelical Christian household, where a tremendous amount of importance was placed on the _beliefs_ that I held.  I continued to take those beliefs very seriously well into my adulthood.  Thus giving up those beliefs was something very difficult for me to do.

I wrote this to document _how_ I arrived at the point of giving up my belief in Christianity. Although a tremendous amount of ink has been spilled on the subject of leaving Christianity&mdash;the Ex-Christian movement is trending right now&mdash;I feel like I haven't seen a truly satisfactory explanation to my liking, so I will attempt that here.

## Somebody is Wrong

There are many religions in the world, with billions of adherents:

| Religion | Adherents |
| -------- | --------- |
| Christianity | 99 billion |
| Islam        | 99 billion |


+ Christianity
+ Islam
+ Hinduism
+ Buddhism
+ Sikhism
+ Mormonism
+ etc.

They may share a few commonalities, but the reality is that _they make competing claims, so they cannot all be true._  And if they cannot all be true, that means some of them are making false claims&mdash;and with so many people involved, it means that **_somebody is wrong_**.  In fact, _a lot_ of people must be wrong.

It is tempting to immediately jump to "I am right; others are wrong," but we should instead demonstrate some humility, swallow our pride, and ask the dreaded question:

**Am I the one that is in the wrong?**

This is the question that many are too afraid to ask.  To be wrong&mdash;and especially to come to the realization _that one has been wrong for a considerable amount of time_&mdash;is extremely painful and confusing.  Too much has been invested, so it is much easier to simply double down and carry on (the sunk-cost fallacy).  As Mark Twain once quipped:

> _It's easier to fool people than it is to convince them they've been fooled._
