Why I Wrote This

I grew up in an Evangelical Christian household, where a tremendous amount of importance was placed on the beliefs that I held. I continued to take those beliefs very seriously well into my adulthood. Thus giving up those beliefs was something very difficult for me to do.

I wrote this to document how I arrived at the point of giving up my belief in Christianity. Although a tremendous amount of ink has been spilled on the subject of leaving Christianity—the Ex-Christian movement is trending right now—I feel like I haven’t seen a truly satisfactory explanation to my liking, so I will attempt that here.

Somebody is Wrong

There are many religions in the world, with billions of adherents:

  • Christianity
  • Islam
  • Hinduism
  • Buddhism
  • Sikhism
  • Mormonism
  • etc.

They may share a few commonalities, but the truth is that they make many competing claims, so they cannot all be true. And if they cannot all be true, that means some of them are making false claims—and with so many people involved, it means that somebody is wrong. In fact, a lot of people must be wrong.

It is tempting to immediately jump to “I am right; others are wrong,” but we should instead demonstrate some humility, and ask the dreaded question:

Am I wrong?

This is the question most are too afraid to ask. To be wrong—and especially to come to the realization that one has been wrong for a considerable amount of time—is extremely painful and confusing. Too much has been invested, so it is much easier to simply double down and carry on (the sunk-cost fallacy). As Mark Twain once quipped:

It’s easier to fool people than it is to convince them they’ve been fooled.
